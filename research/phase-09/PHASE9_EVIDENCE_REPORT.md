# ANDROMEDA-5 / PHASE 9

## Experimental Falsification Gate: Published Torsion-Balance Evidence

**Research date:** 11 November 2026  
**Status:** Unreviewed theoretical and secondary-data analysis. **No detection** of a new scalar force and **no faster-than-light observation**.

### Executive determination

**Phase 9 advances from hypothetical simulations to actual, previously published measurements.** We found the 2020 University of Washington John G. Lee dissertation *A Fourier-Bessel Test of the Gravitational Inverse-Square Law*. Its Appendix A.1 (printed pp. 147-150) publishes a table of corrected, calibrated measurements. We transcribed **95 instrument runs × 3 harmonic torques = 285 torque values**, with reported uncertainties and surface separation. We also checked its published Newtonian goodness-of-fit and the dissertation's single-Yukawa profile results in Table 11.4.

The outcome is an *experimental screen*, not an exact exclusion of ANDROMEDA-5. The paper reports no significant additional short-range force. Our two-pole model has not yet been convolved with the as-built instrument geometry or tested using its joint, nuisance-profiled likelihood. An exclusion or discovery claim would currently be unjustified.

### 2 | Direct checks on the published measurements

For 14 measurements with listed separations between 68.0 and 70.2 µm, we fitted a **local weighted straight line** to each torque harmonic, reporting the intercept at 69.0 µm. This is a descriptive repeatability calculation, not a fundamental-force fit.

| Torque harmonic | Intercept at 69 µm (fN m) | Formal statistical error (fN m) | Local chi-square / 12 dof |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 120ω | 1.46205 | 0.00877 | 7.60 / 12 |
| 18ω | 1.04942 | 0.00513 | 21.16 / 12 |
| 54ω | 0.11745 | 0.00448 | 12.83 / 12 |

The different harmonic fit residuals warn against treating errors as independent and fully characterized. In particular, the 18ω local residual statistic is less comfortable than the others under a naïve independent-noise assumption. This is **not a new force**: the local regression does not use the Newtonian torque prediction, common calibration covariance, or the full apparatus systematics.

The thesis reports that its **complete** Newtonian model gave chi-square **274.99 for 285 degrees of freedom**. Independent evaluation of the chi-square survival function gives **p = 0.65405**, agreeing with the research paper's reported **0.654**. **This check reproduces the published probability from its stated test statistic; it does not independently reproduce the original Newtonian torque model.**

### 3 | The exact ANDROMEDA-5 model to test

Our *hypothetical* scalar dispersion is

$$\omega^2=c^2\big(k^2+\ell^2k^4+\mu^2\big).$$

For the Phase 8 illustrative choices $\ell=2\times10^{-4}$ m and $\mu=1000$ m$^{-1}$, define $\Delta=1-4\ell^2\mu^2$ and

$$a^2={1-\sqrt\Delta\over 2\ell^2},\qquad b^2={1+\sqrt\Delta\over 2\ell^2}.$$

The static spatial kernel has the *difference-of-Yukawas* form

$$G(r)={e^{-ar}-e^{-br}\over 4\pi r\sqrt\Delta}.$$

Calculated pole lengths: **$1/a=0.978906$ mm**, **$1/b=0.204310$ mm**. They are assumed model scales, not measured interaction ranges.

A torque calculation cannot be replaced by the point-body kernel or an infinite-slab approximation. It must integrate the density/charge maps of the rotating test masses and their shield, calculate the **18ω, 54ω and 120ω harmonic responses**, and profile the shared instrumental nuisance parameters.

### 4 | What the published single-Yukawa fits can and cannot tell us

Table 11.4 of the dissertation gives separately profiled **single-Yukawa** fit estimates at many ranges. At ranges bracketing our two poles, log-range interpolation gives approximately:

| Single-Yukawa component only | Interpolated strength $\hat\alpha$ | Quoted 1-sigma scale |
|---|---:|---:|
| Long, 0.978906 mm | -0.00577 | 0.00805 |
| Short, 0.204310 mm | -0.00261 | 0.01548 |

These are **not** jointly estimated coefficients for the ANDROMEDA-5 potential. Correlations between the templates and shared nuisance fits are unknown.

For an *illustrative Gaussian linearized model only*, we varied an unknown template overlap correlation from -0.95 to +0.95. The resulting proxy absolute significance was between **0.42 and 1.18**, depending on this **assumed** correlation. No 95% confidence bound, combined detection statistic, or allowed coupling interval for the actual experiment follows from this exercise. It merely illustrates how much the missing joint response matrix matters. Its graph and source code are supplied separately.

### 5 | Adversarial verdict

**Accepted as real data:** The independent experiment measured the listed torques and reported consistency with Newtonian gravity over its studied range.

**Accepted as computation:** We created a transparent transcription and independently computed local summary fits, the published chi-square probability, and a sensitivity calculation using marginal Yukawa estimates.

**Not established:** A new force, a physically existing scalar field, an electron coupling, superluminal signal transmission, or a model-specific exclusion of the ANDROMEDA-5 interaction.

The major missing element is **the specific instrument's two-pole harmonic response and a joint likelihood**. It is not honest to map the published single-Yukawa 38.6 µm headline limit onto our difference-of-Yukawas model without performing that computation.

### 6 | Phase 10: precise next experimental gate

1. **Verify transcription against page images** and resolve the discrepancy between nominal minimum published separation and a few Appendix A entries. Trace the run selection and separation-offset convention before fitting.
2. **Reconstruct Fourier-Bessel torque templates** for each Yukawa pole using detector/attractor geometry, densities, foil thicknesses, angular offsets, surface corrections, magnetic contamination and calibration uncertainties from the dissertation.
3. **Reproduce the authors' Newtonian fit**, including their stated chi-square and degrees of freedom, from the listed 95×3 values using the same systematic and covariance model. A successful independent fit is the prerequisite to any new-theory limit.
4. **Jointly fit the two-pole signal**, profiling the common nuisance parameters. Report a confidence region for overall coupling, pole scales and material response with coverage tests. Keep all trials and failures.
5. **Submit the implementation to independent experimentalists**. Request the underlying `.sum` time-series files, the template lookup tables and the calibration covariance where publicly accessible information is inadequate.
6. **Only then** develop a real electron-field emission and detection experiment. Even an observed static force would not establish faster-than-light messaging.
