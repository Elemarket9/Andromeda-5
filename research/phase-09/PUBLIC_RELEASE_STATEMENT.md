# Public communication — ANDROMEDA-5 Phase 9

**Title:** ANDROMEDA-5: Testing a hypothetical two-range force against published short-range gravity measurements

The ANDROMEDA-5 research project has completed a computational sensitivity analysis using fit summaries published in John G. Lee's 2020 University of Washington dissertation for the Eöt-Wash short-range gravity experiment. The project investigates a hypothetical scalar field whose static potential contains a fixed difference of two Yukawa terms with ranges approximately 0.979 mm and 0.204 mm.

Using four relevant published fit-summary rows, and assuming an idealized common linear-Gaussian likelihood, the two-pole significance remains below 1.4 standard deviations across tested correlation assumptions and adjacent source-row pairings. This does **not** constitute a direct refit of the experimental torque data, because the required joint covariance, full apparatus response and nuisance-profile information are not available in the current analysis.

**No new force, faster-than-light communication or violation of established physics has been observed.** We are making the derivation, source ledger, source code and calculations available for independent review, and are seeking instrument-level data to attempt an exact joint-likelihood test of the hypothesis.

References: Lee et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 101101 (2020), DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.101101; Lee, *A Fourier-Bessel Test of the Gravitational Inverse-Square Law*, University of Washington dissertation (2020), Table 11.4.
